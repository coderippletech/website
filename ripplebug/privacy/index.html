Ripplebug Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026 · Applies to the Ripplebug browser extension, the Ripplebug app for iPhone and Android, the Ripplebug Board service at board.coderippletech.com, and the RippleBug for Jira app.

The short version

Ripplebug exists to move bug reports from your browser to the places you choose — and nowhere else. The extension has no analytics and no tracking. It talks to our servers only to sign you in to Jira (see below) and, if you connect a Ripplebug Board, to save reports to it. The phone app sends reports only to your board. The Board service stores only what you put on it. None of it is used for advertising or tracking, and you can delete your account at any time.

The extension

  • Credentials (Azure DevOps PAT, GitHub/GitLab/Trello tokens, Jira/Linear API keys, Slack/Teams webhook URLs, board device tokens) are stored in your browser's local extension storage. They are sent only to the service they belong to, directly from your browser. They never touch our servers. Jira tokens, the Jira API token and test-tool keys (Xray, Zephyr, AIO Tests) are also encrypted at rest with a key that stays in your browser.
  • Sign in with Atlassian (Jira): Atlassian requires a server-held secret to issue and renew sign-in tokens, so our server makes that exchange and passes the tokens straight back to your browser through a single-use code that expires after two minutes. Renewals go through the same server. We keep no copy of the tokens and never see your bug reports. Using a Jira API token instead skips our server entirely.
  • Captures (screenshots, screen recordings, console and network logs) stay on your device until you file a report — at which point they are sent only to the destination(s) you selected for that report. Values that look like secrets (bearer tokens, API keys, passwords, secret URL parameters) are replaced with [redacted] before anything leaves your browser.
  • Jira issues also get a small ripplebug issue property (page address, browser and OS, viewport, steps, file names, log counts, and the test or story the bug came from) so RippleBug for Jira can show the evidence. It is stored on the issue in your own Jira site. When you pick a failed test, the defect is recorded in that test tool using your own key.
  • Console & network capture runs via a lightweight page logger on tabs you browse; the data is kept in a small in-memory buffer and is discarded unless you attach it to a report.
  • The extension contains no analytics, no ads, and no third-party trackers.

The phone app (iPhone and Android)

  • Sign-in: you sign in on the board's own page. The app keeps the resulting sign-in token in the phone's secure storage (Keychain on iPhone, Keystore on Android).
  • Screenshots and recordings: the app only handles what you pick, share to it, or record with its screen recorder. Nothing is uploaded until you file the report; until then the draft stays on the phone. Recordings have no audio. Markup and blur are applied on the phone before upload.
  • Photos access (optional) lets the app show the screenshots and recordings you took in the last 30 minutes, so you can report one in a tap. It reads them on the phone and uploads only the ones you file.
  • Device details: each report carries the phone's model, operating system and version, the app version, screen size and language, so the bug can be reproduced.
  • Notifications (optional): if you turn them on, the app gives the board a push token so it can tell you when your report moves or gets a reply. Notifications are delivered through Expo's push service and Apple Push Notification service or Firebase Cloud Messaging. Turning them off, or signing out, removes the token.
  • The app contains no analytics, no ads, and no third-party trackers.

The Board service (board.coderippletech.com)

  • Account data: your email address (or GitHub identity if you sign in with GitHub) and a password hash. We do not sell or share it.
  • Content: captures, media files, comments, and board structure you create are stored on our backend infrastructure (Convex) so they can sync in real time across your devices.
  • Guest reports: if you share a guest link, reports submitted through it (including the reporter's name if they provide one, and their browser / viewport details) are stored on your board. Guest links are revocable at any time.
  • Share links: content behind a share link is visible to anyone who has the link, until you disable it.
  • Send-to connections: if you connect a tracker for exporting cards from the board, those credentials are stored in the service database to perform exports on your behalf, encrypted at rest. Disconnect at any time to remove them. Connecting Jira with Atlassian sign-in stores the sign-in tokens (encrypted) and the Jira site, but not your Atlassian account id or name.
  • AI features (Pro boards: triage, and "Describe it for me" in the phone app) send the report's text, logs and the images you chose to our AI provider, Anthropic, to write the summary. Anthropic does not use it to train models. Boards without AI send nothing.

RippleBug for Jira (the Atlassian app)

  • It shows the evidence of bugs filed with Ripplebug inside the Jira issue. It reads issues and attachments as the person viewing them; nothing is copied out of Jira.
  • If a site admin connects a board, the app keeps the board's sign-in tokens in Atlassian's Forge storage and asks the board for the card linked from an issue (its column, assignee, comment count and AI summary).
  • It sends our server install and uninstall events and weekly counts of bugs filed with Ripplebug on the site. Numbers only: no issue content, attachments or names.

Data deletion

Deleting a capture removes its media from storage. Deleting a board removes all of its content.

You can delete your account at any time, in Settings on the board or with Delete account in the phone app. That removes your account, your sign-ins, and every board you own with all its cards, files and comments, and cancels any paid plan. Cards and comments you added to other people's boards stay on those boards, shown as "Deleted user". You can also email us and we will do it for you.

Permissions the extension requests

  • Access to sites — required to screenshot the visible tab and to talk directly to the tracker APIs you connect.
  • Tab capture / scripting — required for screen recording and the console/network logger.
  • Storage — for your settings and drafts, locally.
  • Identity — to open Atlassian's sign-in page for Sign in with Atlassian and receive the result.

Permissions the phone app requests

  • Photos: to pick screenshots and recordings, and (if you allow it) to show the ones you just took.
  • Screen recording: asked by the phone each time you start a recording; only while it runs.
  • Notifications: optional, for updates on your reports.
