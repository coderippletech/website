RippleReach Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 10, 2026 · Applies to RippleReach for Windows and macOS, RippleReach Host on the Mac, the RippleReach AI agent tools, and RippleReach for iPhone, iPad and Android. Published by CodeRipple Tech Ltd ("we", "us").

The short version

RippleReach connects your devices to your own computers, directly. Your connections, passwords and sessions stay on your devices and the computers you connect to; they never pass through our servers. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. The desktop app talks to the internet for two things only: checking a licence with our payment provider, and checking for updates.

What stays on your device

  • Your library — computers, folders, settings, tags, notes, the last picture of each computer and when you last connected — is a database on the device you use RippleReach on.
  • Passwords are kept in the operating system's own secure storage: Windows Credential Manager, the macOS or iOS Keychain, or the Android Keystore. They are sent only to the computer they belong to, when you connect to it. The iOS Keychain items are not synced to iCloud.
  • Certificate fingerprints of the computers you trust, so RippleReach can warn you if one changes.

Sessions

A session is an encrypted connection (Remote Desktop's TLS and network-level authentication) from your device straight to the computer you chose, on your network or over your own VPN. The screen, sound, keyboard and mouse input, clipboard text and any files or folders you choose to share travel only between those two. We don't see, relay or record any of it. To show which computers are online, RippleReach may briefly connect to their Remote Desktop port; to find Macs and PCs nearby, it listens for their announcements on your local network (Bonjour / mDNS).

RippleReach Host (sharing a Mac)

The host runs on your Mac and lets people who sign in with that Mac's account see and control it. It doesn't contact our servers. It needs macOS's Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions to show the screen and take input, and records nothing.

AI agent tools

If you set up the RippleReach agent tools in an AI assistant you use (for example Claude), that assistant can open sessions to computers in your library, see their screens and type into them, on your behalf. The tools run on your computer. Screenshots and text the agent asks for go to that assistant's provider under its own privacy terms; RippleReach keeps a log of the agent's actions on your computer only.

Licences and payments (desktop apps)

Licences are sold and checked by Lemon Squeezy, our payment provider and merchant of record. When you activate or the app re-checks a licence, it sends the licence key and this computer's name to Lemon Squeezy, which returns whether the licence is valid. Payment details are handled entirely by Lemon Squeezy under its privacy policy; we receive the order details (name, email address, what was bought) to provide support. The free trial is tracked on your computer. The phone apps don't check licences.

Updates (desktop apps)

The desktop app checks for new versions on GitHub, where releases are published. GitHub sees the request (your IP address and the app's version) like any download.

Permissions the phone apps ask for

  • Local network (iOS) / Nearby devices (Android 17 and later): to reach the computers you add on your network, and to find nearby ones.
  • Microphone (iPhone and iPad): only if you turn on "Use my microphone" for a connection, so the remote computer can hear you.

The phone apps keep the screen on during a session and use no other personal data: no contacts, location, photos, identifiers or analytics.

Deleting your data

Delete a computer or a saved password in the app, or uninstall the app, and it's gone: we hold no copy. To have your licence and order records removed, email us.

Children

RippleReach is a tool for people who manage computers and isn't directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and anything material is noted in the app's release notes.
